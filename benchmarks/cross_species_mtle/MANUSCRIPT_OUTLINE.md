# Cross-species MTLE: BridgeRNA program-level transfer and functional organization

Source: [Publication review](./PUBLICATION_REVIEW.md) (reviewed 2026-11-02). Proposed placement: supplementary translational stress test.

## Supplementary Methods

- Evaluated whether a frozen BridgeRNA encoder captures corresponding disease-associated transcriptional programs between human mesial temporal lobe epilepsy (MTLE) and phase-specific mouse kainate epilepsy without fitted cross-species alignment.
- Used a fully study-unseen strict cohort: human GSE256068/PRJNA1077986 (58 MTLE and 10 controls) and mouse GSE319769/PRJNA1425071 (6 kainate and 6 matched vehicle/control animals at Day 3 acute, Day 7 intermediate, and Day 14 chronic phases). Mouse phases comprised independent animals rather than longitudinal measurements.
- Constructed natural `log1p(TPM)` inputs in the canonical 15,165-gene order. Human TPM covered 15,141 genes (99.842%; 24 genes zero-filled using established alignment behavior); mouse TPM covered all 15,165 genes and was derived from HTSeq counts using Ensembl-112 exon-union lengths.
- Computed human and phase-specific mouse disease directions from frozen 512-dimensional sample embeddings. Assessed cosine similarity with 5,000 biological-sample bootstrap replicates and 10,000 phenotype-label permutations; raw `log1p(TPM)` and pooled PCA-50 (98.664% cumulative explained variance) were matched baselines.
- Evaluated complementary Bridge representations using L1/L6/L12 contextual gene states, detached cross-fitted Integrated Gradients, and L12 contextual networks. Compared networks with density-matched Pearson and Spearman coexpression and assessed GO, KEGG, and Hallmark co-membership.
- Applied a frozen human GTEx-trained 512→50 Hallmark Ridge head to cached Bridge embeddings. Mouse outputs were zero-shot predictions; pathway effects were disease-minus-matched-control group means. Conventional Hallmark GSEA used the same mouse cohorts, and biological-sample bootstrap intervals quantified Hallmark-head uncertainty.
- Compared frozen Bridge results with pre-mapped findings from Kim et al.; mapping was fixed before querying Bridge outputs. This constitutes literature/method concordance on overlapping source studies rather than independent-cohort replication.

## Supplementary Results

- Bridge produced substantially larger acute cross-species disease-direction point estimates than raw expression or PCA: acute cosine 0.5326 (95% bootstrap CI 0.3885–0.6426) versus 0.0975 for raw expression and 0.0975 for PCA-50. The Bridge point estimates decreased at intermediate (0.2753; 0.1413–0.4270) and chronic (0.2356; -0.0518–0.5730) phases; none exceeded its phenotype-label null (p=0.1888, 0.2448, and 0.3063).
- Neuroinflammation was the strongest literature-concordant program: it was supported by both contextual embeddings and acute IG with compatible direction. TNF signaling received contextual support, while conventional GSEA also identified concordant TNF/NF-kB activity. The final exact-term mapping did not support a broader gliogenesis-replication claim.
- The frozen human GTEx Hallmark head transferred meaningfully to mouse without retraining. Across all 50 Hallmarks, mouse GSEA versus mouse Bridge-head Spearman correlation was 0.6596 acute, 0.3466 intermediate, and 0.5673 chronic; sign agreement was 78%, 92%, and 86%, respectively ([recovery summary](./figures/hallmark_head/mouse_hallmark_recovery_summary.png)).
- Within the strict human MTLE contrast, Bridge retained the raw direction of 35/50 conventional-GSEA Hallmarks (70%) despite compression through the 512-dimensional sample representation and frozen GTEx head. Angiogenesis and Interferon Alpha Response were supported in the same negative direction by both method-specific criteria, indicating partial directional retention rather than complete GSEA replication ([human agreement](./figures/hallmark_head/comparison_options/human_bridge_gsea_agreement.png)).
- Four human pathways were opposite in direction and supported by one method. Bridge-positive Apical Surface, Bile Acid Metabolism, and Fatty Acid Metabolism opposed nonsignificant negative GSEA tendencies, whereas GSEA-supported negative Complement had an unresolved positive Bridge estimate. The lipid-related results are compatible with MTLE lipid remodeling but do not establish increased lipid abundance or Bridge superiority.
- Phase-resolved Bridge-head predictions captured an acute stress/injury state involving unfolded-protein response, p53, interferons, apoptosis, TNF/NF-kB, IL-6/JAK/STAT3, and hypoxia, with several responses weakening by chronic disease. The head additionally resolved dynamic energy/redox behavior—acute suppression followed by intermediate/chronic increases in peroxisome, fatty-acid metabolism, and oxidative phosphorylation—and growth/injury signaling, including intermediate-peaking mTORC1 and attenuating TGF-beta ([phase-resolved programs](./figures/hallmark_head/epilepsy_energy_three_phase_hallmark_states.png)).
- Bridge contextual networks were strongly functionally organized beyond matched coexpression. Bridge-specific top rewiring edges were enriched approximately 3.8–4.2-fold for GO, 4.6–5.0-fold for KEGG, and 14.1–14.6-fold for Hallmark co-membership after removing matched Pearson/Spearman top edges.
- Cross-species agreement was concentrated at the program/organization level rather than individual genes or edges. DE and IG were weakly negative in acute/intermediate phases, no L12 genes passed joint conserved/discordant criteria, and signed network-rewiring cosines were near zero (-0.00093, 0.00128, and 0.00182). The complete human–mouse Hallmark-head vectors were also negative and nonsignificant (cosines -0.484, -0.420, and -0.350; family q=0.870).
- Continuous IG features produced a small held-out prioritization gain over an unsigned conventional-DE baseline: AUPRC increased by 0.0075–0.0137 across phases and by 0.0128 in the pooled analysis, although AUROC decreased by 0.0035–0.0073. The pooled adjusted odds ratio for opposite IG sign was 1.49 (gene-cluster-robust 95% CI 1.37–1.62).

## Supplementary Discussion

- The benchmark demonstrates that frozen Bridge representations can retain transferable higher-order pathway structure across species: a Hallmark head trained only on human GTEx broadly recovered the mouse epilepsy pathway landscape and exposed phase-specific stress, metabolic, and growth/injury states without mouse retraining.
- Bridge’s strongest translational signal was multiscale but program-specific. Neuroinflammation was recovered through two distinct frozen Bridge views, and contextual networks retained substantial functional organization beyond conventional coexpression even when individual-gene and signed rewiring conservation were weak.
- The results support a distinction between conserved biological programs and species-specific molecular implementation. Human and mouse may engage related inflammatory or injury-response programs while differing in the genes, attribution patterns, and network edges through which those programs are realized.
- Agreement between the Hallmark head and conventional GSEA is transfer/recovery evidence rather than fully independent validation because the head was trained against expression-derived ssGSEA-like targets. Its human–mouse global vector remained discordant, so mouse pathway recovery must not be interpreted as proof of conserved human disease state or mechanism.
- The human comparison suggests attenuation rather than wholesale loss: most pathway directions are retained, but shared statistical support is sparse. Bridge-only positive lipid-related scores may reflect a higher-order remodeling state not expressed as coordinated rank enrichment, while Apical Surface and Bile Acid Metabolism require nonliteral interpretations as polarity/barrier and lipid/sterol programs.
- Interpretation is limited by one strict study per species, six animals per mouse condition, surgical MTLE cases versus older post-mortem controls, predominantly MTLE-HS disease, and acquisition/cellular-composition differences. The focused Hallmark phase figure is descriptive: phases use independent animals, and Bridge bootstrap support is not equivalent to GSEA FDR support.
- Overall, Bridge provided the clearest added value as a zero-shot functional-state and biological-organization framework rather than as a validated predictor of global human–mouse disease-direction alignment. The benchmark therefore supports a focused supplementary claim about transferable program-level structure while preserving the null primary endpoint.

## Suggested main-text reference

In a fully study-unseen human–mouse MTLE stress test, frozen Bridge representations showed suggestive acute cross-species geometry and recovered neuroinflammatory program concordance, while a human GTEx-trained Hallmark head transferred zero-shot to the mouse kainate model and broadly tracked its phase-resolved GSEA landscape. Global human–mouse correspondence did not exceed phenotype-label nulls, indicating that the strongest transferable signal lay in higher-order program organization rather than conserved individual genes or disease-network rewiring (Supplementary analysis).
