# Frozen Hallmark-state readout for retrieval and cross-species interpretation

Source: [consolidated publication review](PUBLICATION_REVIEW.md) and the copied [Hallmark-state retrieval evidence](references/hallmark_state_retrieval_agent/README.md).

## Methods

- **Training targets:** use 4,260 GTEx samples from 920 donors and 12 broad tissues; rank all 15,165 canonical genes within each sample, normalize ranks by 15,165, average ranks among the canonical members of each of 50 MSigDB Hallmarks, and standardize each Hallmark across GTEx.
- **Frozen head:** represent each sample by the unweighted final-layer mean-pooled 512-dimensional Bridge embedding; fit a GTEx `StandardScaler` and 50-output Ridge model, selecting one global alpha from `0.01, 0.1, 1, 10, 100` by five-fold donor-grouped cross-validation (`alpha=0.1`), then refit and freeze the complete system.
- **Inference semantics:** compute `((z − GTEx mean) / GTEx scale)Wᵀ + b` after authoritative natural `log1p(TPM)` preprocessing and exact canonical ordering. Outputs are continuous predictions of GTEx-standardized rank-mean Hallmark targets—not probabilities, GSEA NES values, formal ssGSEA, causal activities, decoder membership scores, or IG contributions.
- **Independent validation:** compare frozen Bridge Hallmark profiles with expression-derived rank-mean Hallmark profiles across 9,816 patient-independent TCGA primary tumors and 32 cancer types; quantify cancer-profile correlation, cancer-similarity geometry, and pathway-wise cancer rankings.
- **Human retrieval:** query 510,709 human ARCHS4 samples with individual TCGA tumors and cancer centroids in Bridge-512 and Hallmark-50 raw-cosine spaces; evaluate neighborhood overlap, exact disease, tissue, malignancy, related-cancer coherence, study diversity, hubs, pathway coverage, and dominant-pathway robustness. Include a centroid-only natural-`log1p(TPM)` baseline on the same 40,000 candidates.
- **Extended retrieval controls:** evaluate 9,942 tumors across 33 cancers including LAML, focused LUAD leakage, whole-vector Hallmark cosine, signed top-5/top-10 pathway overlap, rank-based matching, and query-containment rules without refitting Bridge or the Hallmark head.
- **Cross-species boundary:** apply the identical human GTEx-trained head to 1,471 eligible mouse OSDR samples and query human-only, mouse-only, and combined ARCHS4 references (510,709 human + 429,746 mouse); preserve exact FLT/GC groups and use 1,000 within-group label permutations for differential retrieval.

## Results

- **Independent biological validation:** conventional-expression versus Bridge-Hallmark cancer profiles had mean Pearson `0.756`; conventional versus Bridge-Hallmark cancer-similarity geometry had Spearman `0.862`, compared with `0.388` for conventional versus raw Bridge-512; median pathway-wise cancer-ranking Spearman was `0.824` across 50 Hallmarks.
- **Distinct search geometry:** Bridge/Hallmark median Jaccard@100 was `0.111` for 9,816 individual queries (median overlap `20/100`) and `0.124` for 32 cancer centroids (`22/100`); `74.6%` of Hallmark centroid top-100 results were absent from the Bridge top 100.
- **Centroid-level utility:** across 33 cancer centroids, exact-disease P@10 was `0.482` for Hallmark and `0.488` for Bridge, tissue P@10 was `0.512` versus `0.506`, and malignancy P@10 was `0.855` versus `0.864`. Hallmark whole-vector cosine increased signed pathway coverage@10 from Bridge's `0.707` to `0.791`.
- **Individual-level tradeoff:** macro patient-level P@50 favored Bridge for exact disease (`0.338` versus Hallmark `0.305`), tissue (`0.424` versus `0.395`), malignancy (`0.777` versus `0.707`), and disease-or-related-cancer (`0.353` versus `0.313`). Hallmark is therefore not a replacement for global retrieval.
- **Functional examples and sparse matching:** UVM and CHOL Hallmark centroids returned `8/10` and `9/10` exact hits versus `6/10` for Bridge, and LUAD retrieved several LUSC samples with shared lung-cancer programs. Signed top-10 overlap raised pathway coverage to `0.888` but reduced exact-disease P@10 to `0.388`, demonstrating the identity-versus-program tradeoff.
- **OSDR reproducibility and specificity:** Hallmark replicate neighbor Jaccard was approximately `0.613–0.629` versus `0.366–0.386` for Bridge, but Hallmark retained much less tissue context and was more hub-sensitive. Strict FLT-enriched retrieval occurred in 24/88 groups rather than defining a universal spaceflight state.
- **Species conditioning:** combined top-10 mouse fractions were `99.53%` for Bridge and `100%` for both Hallmark methods; species nearest-neighbor purity was `0.9976` and `0.9993`. Adding mouse references increased Hallmark tissue coherence from `0.0633` in forced human-only retrieval to `0.6352`, showing that unconstrained retrieval primarily selects same-species references.

## Discussion

- The strongest positive result is not retrieval superiority: it is that a frozen linear readout converts Bridge's pooled 512-dimensional representation into a named pathway coordinate system that reproduces independently calculated TCGA Hallmark organization.
- Bridge-512 and Hallmark-50 answer different questions. Bridge is the stronger default for global transcriptomic, tissue, disease, and experimental-context similarity; Hallmark is a secondary view for shared predicted functional programs.
- Cancer-centroid Hallmark retrieval is approximately competitive with Bridge and more pathway-focused, while individual-patient retrieval exposes information loss from the `512 → 50` compression.
- Selected UVM, CHOL, LUAD/LUSC, adipose, and muscle results illustrate functional reranking, but generic proliferation, DNA-repair, UPR, heme, and stress programs can connect unrelated contexts.
- Cross-species use requires explicit reference conditioning. A combined human/mouse index is not a neutral analogy engine because species dominates nearest-neighbor structure; human-only search is a forced mouse→human comparison.
- The validated operational formulation is two-view retrieval: use Bridge-512 for global context and Hallmark-50 for interpretable functional hypotheses, accompanied by hub, study, metadata, pathway-dominance, leakage, and species controls.

## Suggested Results paragraph

We trained a frozen 50-output Hallmark readout on 4,260 GTEx samples from 920 donors using independently derived, GTEx-standardized rank-mean targets and donor-grouped Ridge selection (`alpha=0.1`). Applied unchanged to 9,816 TCGA tumors, the readout reproduced independent expression-derived cancer pathway organization: mean cancer-profile Pearson was `0.756`, cancer-similarity geometry Spearman was `0.862` versus `0.388` for raw Bridge-512, and median pathway-wise cancer-ranking Spearman was `0.824`. Searching 510,709 human ARCHS4 samples produced a distinct functional neighborhood—median Bridge/Hallmark Jaccard@100 was `0.111` for individual queries and `0.124` for cancer centroids—while centroid exact-disease precision remained nearly tied (`0.482` Hallmark versus `0.488` Bridge) and signed pathway coverage increased from `0.707` to `0.791`. Individual-patient disease, tissue, and malignancy precision nevertheless favored Bridge, establishing Hallmark-50 as a complementary functional-state view rather than a replacement for global retrieval. In a combined 940,455-sample human/mouse atlas, mouse OSDR queries retrieved `99.53–100%` mouse neighbors at top 10, demonstrating that cross-species interpretation requires explicitly conditioned reference sets.
