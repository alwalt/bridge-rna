# Frozen Bridge retrieval geometry and reference-only whitening

Source: [publication review](PUBLICATION_REVIEW.md), restricted to the user-selected positive findings.

## Methods

- Evaluate frozen BridgeRNA sample embeddings using 562 exact ARCHS4↔recount3 GSM pairs from 40 repository-defined `unseen_study` GSEs, searched against 510,709 human ARCHS4 reference samples; treat these as cross-pipeline technical pairs rather than independent biological replicates.
- Compare absolute representation agreement with retrieval uniqueness using exact-pair cosine, R@1/5/10/100/1,000, MRR, exact rank, sample-micro summaries, and equal-weight study-macro summaries.
- Characterize ARCHS4 embedding geometry using 2,000,000 deterministic random distinct sample pairs, the global mean direction, covariance eigenspectrum, participation ratio, local density, and hubness.
- Apply representation post-processing fitted exclusively on the 510,709 ARCHS4 references: mean-centering, frozen ABTT D=10, and full 512-dimensional covariance whitening with eigenvalue floor `lambda_max × 1e-12`; apply the resulting transforms unchanged to recount3 and GTEx queries, with no Bridge training or fine-tuning.
- Evaluate biological retrieval with 6,064 eligible GTEx v11 queries spanning 13 harmonized tissues against 510,376 ARCHS4 references. Before scoring, conservatively exclude 333 GTEx-associated GEO records identified from explicit metadata: 296 simulated-read records carrying GTEx donor identifiers and 37 GEO/ENCODE assays described as using GTEx-derived tissue material. These exclusions are provenance safeguards, not evidence that the records are exact duplicates of the GTEx v11 queries. Report annotated precision, absolute confirmed hit rate, unknown rate, enrichment, and equal-weight tissue-macro metrics.
- Run a fully labeled GTEx→GTEx control after excluding each query and every sample from the same donor; summarize tissue retrieval across 6,064 queries from a cohort of 937 donors.

## Results

- Exact ARCHS4↔recount3 counterparts showed strong cross-pipeline agreement: mean cosine was 0.997806 and median cosine was 0.999218 across 562 pairs, compared with a median of 0.912954 among 2,000,000 sampled random ARCHS4 pairs ([paired cosine summary](results/absolute_similarity/paired_cosine_micro_macro_summary.csv); [random-versus-exact summary](results/absolute_similarity/random_vs_exact_cosine.csv)).
- High absolute agreement did not guarantee unique retrieval. Raw Bridge cosine achieved R@1 28.47%, R@10 77.22%, MRR 0.4764, and median exact rank 2; 33 queries still had rank >100 with exact-pair cosine >0.99 ([ranked methods](results/ranked_method_comparison.csv); [high-similarity poor-rank frequency](results/absolute_similarity/high_similarity_poor_rank_frequency.csv)).
- The raw embedding space was strongly anisotropic: mean random-pair cosine was 0.878748, while the squared magnitude of the unit mean direction was 0.878774. Mean-centering shifted median random-pair cosine from 0.912954 to −0.002471, quantitatively linking the high raw baseline to a shared global direction ([anisotropy summary](results/anisotropy/anisotropy_summary.csv); [cosine distributions](results/anisotropy/cosine_distribution_summaries.csv)).
- ARCHS4-fitted whitening substantially improved exact retrieval without representation learning: R@1 increased from 28.47% to 46.62%, R@10 from 77.22% to 94.48%, R@100 from 91.81% to 99.11%, MRR from 0.4764 to 0.6919, and study-macro R@10 from 75.92% to 94.12% ([literature comparison](results/literature_postprocessing/main_comparison.csv)).
- Whitening also improved GTEx→ARCHS4 tissue retrieval within Bridge: tissue-macro P@10 increased from 86.73% with raw cosine to 91.21%, and macro absolute Hit@10 increased from 23.13% to 24.77%. Approximately 74% of Top-10 references remained unlabeled under the conservative ARCHS4 mapping, so this cross-dataset result is supporting evidence and annotated precision is interpreted together with Hit@10 and Unknown@10 ([GTEx tissue-macro metrics](results/gtex_biological_retrieval/retrieval_metrics_tissue_macro.csv)).
- In the fully labeled different-donor GTEx→GTEx control, whitening increased tissue-macro P@10 from 90.61% to 94.86% and micro P@10 from 92.28% to 95.65%, supporting a biological-neighborhood gain beyond exact GSM matching ([GTEx control](results/gtex_biological_retrieval/gtex_to_gtex_control.csv)).

## Discussion

- Bridge strongly preserves sample identity across independent ARCHS4 and recount3 processing, but absolute similarity and exact retrieval rank answer different questions: highly concordant pairs can remain difficult to identify uniquely in crowded transcriptomic neighborhoods.
- The close match between mean random-pair cosine and the squared mean-direction magnitude shows that Bridge’s high ordinary cosine baseline is primarily a geometric anisotropy effect; raw cosine values should therefore be interpreted relative to the empirical Bridge background rather than by generic thresholds.
- Full reference-only whitening exposes substantially more discriminative frozen Bridge geometry across R@K, MRR, and equal-study evaluation, without fitting on recount3 or GTEx and without modifying Bridge parameters.
- The GTEx→ARCHS4 and different-donor GTEx→GTEx results indicate that the whitening gain extends to broad tissue organization. The ARCHS4 result remains conditional on sparse conservative tissue annotations and conservative removal of GTEx-associated GEO records; exact duplication of the GTEx v11 query cohort was not established. The fully labeled donor-excluded GTEx control provides the cleaner biological validation.
- These findings support raw cosine for interpretable absolute representation agreement and ARCHS4-fitted whitening for retrieval ranking. Whitening should be described as post hoc geometry correction, not evidence that Bridge is batch-corrected.

This geometry was strongly anisotropic—the squared unit mean-direction magnitude was 0.878774, closely matching mean random-pair cosine 0.878748—and mean-centering moved median random similarity to −0.002471. A full 512-dimensional whitening transform fitted only on ARCHS4 references increased exact R@1 to 46.62%, R@10 to 94.48%, R@100 to 99.11%, and study-macro R@10 to 94.12%. The improvement extended to biological retrieval: GTEx→ARCHS4 tissue-macro P@10 increased from 86.73% to 91.21%, while donor-excluded GTEx→GTEx tissue-macro P@10 increased from 90.61% to 94.86%. The ARCHS4 search conservatively removed 333 GTEx-associated GEO records—296 simulated-read records and 37 assays on GTEx-derived material—without claiming that they were exact duplicates of the GTEx v11 queries. Because approximately 74% of retrieved ARCHS4 neighbors lacked conservative tissue labels, the fully labeled donor-excluded GTEx control provides the clearest support that reference-only whitening improves the organization of frozen Bridge representations beyond exact sample matching.
